# Five Lamps — the hidden views (draft 2, checked)

Five small codes are hidden in the wall's art. Each one opens the same story from someone standing inside it. These are told voices: imagined from within each tradition, never a quotation, and never in the voice of the Guru.

## 6 · Satyabhama — behind the chariot (story 1)
I took the reins because someone had to. Krishna had fallen beside me; the arrows were mine.

They say I was the Earth before I was a queen. I did not remember that. What I remember is that when I raised the bow, my hands knew him before my eyes did. Not as an enemy. As someone who had wandered too far from home.

A mother does not always get to choose how she brings a child back. Sometimes the only door left is the one you would never have chosen.

When he fell, he looked at me, and I saw him remember. He asked for lamps. I lit the first one myself.

## 7 · Shiva — the one who held the poison (story 2)
Everyone pulled on the rope for treasure. Nobody pulled for what would come up first.

When the dark rose out of the ocean, the gods stepped back and the asuras stepped back, and for a moment there was nothing between the world and the poison but whoever would step forward.

So I drank it. Not all the way down; it stayed here, in my throat, where her hands held it. Swallowed, it would have been the end of me. Spat out, it would have been the end of everything else. Held, it was only blue.

Some things are not for fixing. You can only hold them long enough for the others to keep churning.

## 8 · Gautama — the student sent away (story 3)
He sent me to the next village to teach. I went happily. I thought I would come back and find him exactly where I left him.

The news reached me on the road. I sat down in the dust and I wept like a child, and somewhere in the weeping I heard myself: *my* teacher, *my* light, *my* loss.

That was the last thing I was carrying. Not him. My holding on to him.

I put it down. And the light I had been looking for in his face was already behind my own eyes. He had been pointing at it the whole time.

## 9 · The fifty-third thread (story 4)
*A voice invented for this wall.*

I was not cut for a king.

Fifty-two threads were counted, and fifty-two kings held on. I was the one at the end, a little longer than the rest, and nobody's hand was on me when the cloak moved.

I swung behind them all the way out of the fort, into the open, into the light of the city. No one pulled me. I came anyway.

Maybe I was spare. Or maybe someone was always going to be standing here, at this wall, a long way from that fort, reading this.

Go on. Hold on.

## 10 · A lamp-lighter in Ayodhya (story 5)
*A voice invented for this wall.*

I did not know if it was true. Nobody could be sure; we had heard so many stories in fourteen years.

But it was the darkest night of the month, and my roof was the corner of the road, and I thought: if he is out there, he will need to see the turn.

So I lit one lamp. Then my neighbour saw it and lit hers. By the time I came down the stairs, the whole street was burning gold.

I never saw his face; the crowd was too thick. It did not matter. My lamp was one of the ones that showed him the way.

That is what we do for each other. We do not wait to be sure. We light the turn.

---
*Told voices, imagined: Satyabhama as Bhudevi reborn (South Indian and popular tradition); Shiva's blue throat (the Puranas); Gautama sent to teach and letting go of his attachment (Kalpa Sutra and later Jain tradition); the fifty-third thread and the lamp-lighter are invented for this wall and say so.*
