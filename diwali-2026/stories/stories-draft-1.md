# Five Lamps — the Diwali wall stories (draft 1)

## Opening
We come into this life with a forgetting. Diwali is the night we help each other remember. Five old stories, five lamps. Walk from the dark end to the bright one.

## 1 · Narakasura — The Son Who Forgot
He was the Earth's own child. Bhudevi, the goddess of the ground beneath every foot, bore him, and he was given a gift: no one could end him but his mother.

Somewhere along the way he forgot whose son he was. He built a kingdom out of taking. He took the earrings of Aditi, mother of the gods. He took sixteen thousand women and locked them away. The more he took, the less he remembered, and the darker his city grew.

When Krishna came against him, he did not come alone. Satyabhama rode beside him, and Satyabhama was Bhudevi come again. So it was his mother, in the end, who undid him. The one gift that kept him safe was the same thread that brought him home.

As he fell, Narakasura knew her. His last wish was not for his kingdom. He asked that the day of his ending be kept with lamps, so that people would celebrate the light rather than mourn him.

So the first lamp of Diwali is lit by the one who had been darkest. In many homes it is still lit the day before Diwali, on Naraka Chaturdashi.

Forgetting is not the end of the story. Sometimes it is the start of the way back.

*When did you last lose sight of yourself, and who reminded you?*

## 2 · Lakshmi — What the Churning Brought
The gods had lost their shine. To find it again they had to work with the very ones they feared: gods on one end, asuras on the other, a mountain for a churning rod, a great serpent for a rope, and the whole ocean of milk between them.

They churned for a long time. The first thing to rise was poison, dark enough to end the world. Shiva drank it and held it in his throat, which is why his throat is blue.

They kept churning.

Then treasures began to surface: a cow that grants wishes, a tree that grants wishes, the moon. And at last, standing on a lotus, Lakshmi: abundance, beauty, the good that makes a life full. She looked across both ends of the rope and chose where she would go.

No one churning knew she was in there. They only knew they had to keep going past the poison.

On Diwali night we clean our homes, open our doors and light the way in, so she can find us.

*What came up for you after a hard stretch that you didn't expect?*

## 3 · Mahavira — The Light That Went Inward
Mahavira spent his life learning to see with nothing in the way: no possessions, no harm to any living thing, no clinging. Through his teaching, thousands found their own way to that clear seeing.

On the new-moon night of Kartik, at Pavapuri, he left his body and was freed for good. Jains call it his nirvana.

The kings gathered there understood that a great light had gone out of the world. So they lit lamps. If the light of knowledge had left, they would hold small lights in its place.

His closest disciple, Gautama, was not there; Mahavira had sent him away to teach. When the news reached him he grieved, and in the grieving he saw that he had been holding on to his teacher. He let go. That same night, his own complete knowing opened.

The lamps outside were lit for a teacher who had gone. The light that stayed was the one that had been inside the student all along.

*What's something you understand now that you couldn't see before?*

## 4 · Bandi Chhor — The Cloak with Fifty-Two Tassels
Guru Hargobind, the sixth Sikh Guru, was held in the fort at Gwalior. Inside its walls he met fifty-two kings, prisoners too, some of them there for years.

When the emperor Jahangir agreed to release the Guru, the Guru said he would not leave alone. The emperor set a condition he thought would end the matter: whoever could hold on to the Guru's cloak as he walked out could go with him.

So the Guru had a cloak made with fifty-two tassels. One for every king. They walked out together, each one holding a thread.

He reached Amritsar at Diwali, and the Harmandir Sahib was lit with lamps to welcome him home. Sikhs keep the day as Bandi Chhor Divas: the day of release.

A light you keep for yourself is a lamp. A light you carry out for others becomes a city.

*Who did you bring out of the dark with you?*

## 5 · Rama — Coming Home
For fourteen years Rama lived in the forest, a prince without a palace. Sita was taken from him. He crossed the sea to Lanka, fought, and brought her back.

Then he walked home.

It was the darkest night of the month, the new moon of Kartik, with nothing in the sky to guide anyone. So the people of Ayodhya filled the city with clay lamps, on every roof and wall and road, until the whole city was one light and he could find his way.

Nothing in the forest made him less than he was. It made him remember what he was for.

That is the story every lamp tonight is telling. We are not becoming something new. We are finding our way back to what we already were, and the people who love us light the road.

*What did you forget that you already were, and what brought you back?*
