We call ourselves Sitare, the stars. Not because anyone here performs for a living, but because every one of us takes a turn in the light.

THE FESTIVAL

What is Diwali?

Diwali comes from Deepavali, a row of lamps. It is marked across India and around the world, on the new-moon night of autumn — the darkest night of the month.

That is the whole festival in one picture. On the darkest night, we fill every corner with light.

Dhanteras

Prosperity is welcomed home. Many families bring something new in metal into the house.

Chhoti Diwali

The first lamps are lit. Many remember Krishna's victory over the demon Narakasura.

Diwali · Lakshmi Puja

The main night. Lamps everywhere, and Lakshmi, goddess of prosperity and well-being, is welcomed into every home.

Govardhan Puja

Krishna's sheltering of a whole village is remembered with Annakut, a mountain of food offered in gratitude.

Bhai Dooj

A day for brothers and sisters, and the bond between them.

Customs differ by region and by family. This is one common telling.

THE WAY ARCHANA JI TELLS IT

This season holds the memory of three ages.

Satyug

Lakshmi rises from the churning of the cosmic ocean and is welcomed as abundance itself. It is why we light the way to our doors.

Treta

After fourteen years of exile and the defeat of Ravana, Ram returns to Ayodhya. The people light rows of lamps to guide him home.

Dwapar

Krishna lifts Govardhan hill to shelter the people of Vraj from Indra's storm. Annakut, a mountain of food, is offered in its memory.

AND NOW

Lit far from Ayodhya.

For families living away from where it began, the meaning has grown. It is not only about remembering a homecoming. It is how we make one.

Archana ji adds one more light for our own time: Saraswati, goddess of knowledge, music and the arts, who arrives every year when the stage lights up.

OUR WAY

How Sitare celebrate

The shape is the point. We open in stillness and close in light, and in between, every family takes the stage.

Tap a circle. These are the years behind us.

We open with puja

Before anything else, we gather and pray together. It sets the tone for the whole evening.

Every family takes the stage

Each couple or family brings a signature entry dance. Tell us your song when you RSVP.

Every talent has a place

Bhajans, songs, skits, poems, games. Whatever you bring, there is room for it.

We close with fireworks

The last light of the night belongs to the sky.

For years our people have called this place Sharmaji ka Adda. Devkinandan ji named it Balam Dham, the home of the eternal beloved.
Call it what you will. You do not come here to be an audience. You come to be a Sitara.
